Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) combines large language models with external knowledge sources.
Instead of relying only on parametric memory, RAG retrieves relevant documents at query time.

Semantic chunking improves retrieval quality by grouping text based on meaning rather than fixed token counts.
Adjacent sentences with high embedding similarity stay in the same chunk.
When similarity drops sharply, a new chunk begins.

Cohere embedding models produce dense vectors suitable for semantic search.
The embed-english-v3.0 model outputs 1024-dimensional vectors optimized for retrieval tasks.

Pgvector extends PostgreSQL with vector similarity operators.
Cosine distance is commonly used to rank nearest neighbors for RAG applications.
